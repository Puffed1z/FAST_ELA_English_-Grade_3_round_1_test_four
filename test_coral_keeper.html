The Coral Keeper

by M. J. Carroll
resilient: able to recover quickly from difficulties; strong and flexible
1When the coral reef near the island of Pua began to bleach white, the fishing families were frightened. Without the reef, the fish would go. Without the fish, the families would have nothing to sell or eat. Everyone said the same thing: the reef was dying.
2But eleven-year-old Sela did not believe the reef was dead yet. She had grown up swimming through it — she knew every brain coral and sea fan the way other children knew their own bedrooms.
3"It's stressed," she told her grandmother. "It turned white because the water got too warm. But some of the coral is still alive underneath. I can see it moving."
4Her grandmother listened carefully. "What does it need?"
5"Time. And less disturbance. And maybe new pieces." Sela had read about coral gardening — attaching small living fragments to underwater frames so they could grow and help restore a damaged reef. She asked the village council for permission to try.
6Most of the adults listened politely but said nothing would work. One elder, though, Mr. Puna, said quietly: "The reef has been here longer than any of us. It knows how to survive. Help it remember."
7Sela spent the summer collecting living fragments from the healthier parts of the reef, attaching them to wire frames with the help of her younger brother Kito. The work was slow. The frames were awkward. Some fragments died.
8But by autumn, patches of color had begun to return to the reef — small blushes of pink and orange and green among the white. The water temperature had dropped, and the coral was recovering.
9"You were right," her grandmother said when they snorkeled over it together. "It was only sleeping."
10Sela looked at the returning color. She thought of every fragment she had placed, every frame, every early morning in the water. "It's resilient," she said. "We just had to give it a chance."
"The Coral Keeper" by M. J. Carroll. Original work.
