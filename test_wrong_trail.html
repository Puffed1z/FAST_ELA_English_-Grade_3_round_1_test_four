The Wrong Trail

by M. J. Carroll
summit: the highest point of a mountain or hill
1On the first morning of the hiking trip, Zara took the wrong trail.
2She realized it forty minutes in, when the path narrowed to nothing and ended at the edge of a muddy creek. She stood there for a moment, breathing hard, her backpack suddenly heavier than it had been at the trailhead.
3She could turn back. But turning back meant forty minutes of retracing steps, then however long to reach the summit, which the others in her group had already started toward. She would arrive last. Everyone would know she had gotten lost.
4She pulled out her trail map. The creek was on it — a thin blue line that ran east toward the main trail. If she followed the creek instead of retracing her steps, she could rejoin the group in less time than going back. It was not the planned route. It was not easy terrain. But it was a path.
5Zara stepped into the creek. The water was cold and came up past her ankles. She kept moving.
6An hour later she emerged from the tree line onto the main trail, fifty meters ahead of her group. She sat on a flat rock, ate half a granola bar, and waited.
7When her group appeared around the bend, her friend Obi called out: "You're already here! How?"
8"Shortcut," Zara said.
9It wasn't entirely true. But it wasn't entirely false either.
10That night at camp, she wrote in her journal: The wrong trail taught me more than the right one would have. Next time I'll know to look for the creek.
