The River Pilot

by M. J. Carroll
navigate: to plan and direct a course of travel, especially through difficult conditions
1Tomás had wanted to be a river pilot since the age of seven, when he watched the old pilot, Mr. Brennan, steer a forty-foot barge through the narrowest bend of the Cutter River without slowing down.
2"How did you know where the bottom was?" Tomás asked him afterward.
3"You learn the river," Mr. Brennan said. "Every twist, every sandbar, every season. You don't navigate from the map. You navigate from memory."
4Tomás began spending every afternoon at the river. He watched how the color of the water changed over sandbars — green to pale brown, pale brown to dark. He noticed how the current ran fastest on the outside of each bend and slow and deceptive on the inside. He filled three notebooks.
5At thirteen, he convinced Mr. Brennan to let him help steer on a short run. His hands shook on the wheel. The barge felt enormous — a floating building pressing through the water. But Tomás remembered the colors. He remembered the current. He called the turns before Mr. Brennan did.
6"You've been paying attention," Mr. Brennan said.
7"Every day," said Tomás.
8By the time he was sixteen, Tomás could read the Cutter River the way Mr. Brennan once said — not from a map, but from everything he had ever noticed and remembered. The river had become a language he was fluent in.
9Mr. Brennan retired that spring. "The river's yours now," he said at the dock, and shook Tomás's hand.
10Tomás looked out at the water, wide and brown and full of things only he could see. He nodded. He already knew.
